# Color Science Essentials

## LRV (Light Reflectance Value)

**What it is:** How much light a color reflects (0-100 scale)

**Scale:**
- **0-25:** Very dark
- **25-50:** Medium-dark  
- **50-70:** Medium
- **70-85:** Light
- **85-100:** Very light

**Key Points:**
- Higher LRV = brighter feeling
- Lower LRV = moodier, more intimate
- LRV is ONE factor - not a rule. Dark colors can work anywhere if lighting is right.

## Undertones

**What they are:** The temperature beneath the surface color

**Types:**
- **Warm:** Yellow, orange, red bases → Harmonizes with brass, warm oak, terracotta
- **Cool:** Blue, green, violet bases → Harmonizes with marble, chrome, walnut
- **Neutral:** Balanced, no strong lean

**Undertone Consistency - When It Applies:**

**STRONG GUIDELINE (not absolute law):**
- Connected spaces visible simultaneously → consistent undertones prevent visual discord
- Open floor plans → all spaces should share undertone temperature
- Warm + warm = harmony, Cool + cool = harmony

**CAN BE BENT IF:**
- Client explicitly prefers specific color despite technical clash
- The contrast is intentional (bold design move)
- Client understands tradeoff and accepts the tension

**How to handle:**
"Warm pink + cool slate creates undertone tension. Two approaches:
A. Neutral bridge (harmonious, safer)
B. Embrace contrast (bold, requires confidence)
Which serves your vision?"

**Example:**
- ❌ Blue-gray walls + orange oak floors = undertone clash (but CAN work if client wants it)
- ✅ Warm beige walls + brass + honey oak = natural harmony

## Color Flow & Relationships Between Rooms

### The Core Principle:

**When rooms connect visually, colors must RELATE.** When rooms are separate, they can be independent.

**Visual Connection = Need Color Relationship:**
- Open floor plan (living/dining/kitchen visible together)
- Hallway with views into multiple rooms
- Rooms with open doorways/sightlines

**No Visual Connection = More Freedom:**
- Closed doors between rooms
- Separate floor/wings
- Rooms used at different times

### How to Create Color Flow:

**1. Undertone Consistency (Most Critical)**
- All connected spaces share same undertone temperature
- Warm palette: All colors have warm undertones
- Cool palette: All colors have cool undertones
- Mixed undertones in same sightline = visual discord

**Example:**
- ✅ Hallway (warm beige) → Living (warm gray) → Kitchen (warm green) = flows
- ❌ Hallway (warm beige) → Living (cool blue-gray) = clash

**2. Tonal Progression**
- Colors share undertone but vary in LRV/intensity
- Creates subtle definition without hard stops
- Example: Warm neutral LRV 60 → Warm neutral LRV 50 → Warm green LRV 40

**3. Envelope Effect**
- Same color throughout connected spaces
- Creates maximum flow, sophistication
- Best for: Open plans, color-drenching, small spaces

**4. Accent Strategy**
- Neutral base throughout + bold color in specific zone
- Example: Neutral walls everywhere + bold kitchen cabinets
- Creates interest without breaking flow

### Open Floor Plans:

**Must consider:**
- All "rooms" visible at once
- Colors must relate through undertone OR tonal progression
- High contrast between adjacent spaces = jarring

**Strategies:**
- Single color throughout (envelope)
- Tonal progression (same family, different LRVs)
- Consistent undertone with varied colors

### Closed Floor Plans:

**More flexibility:**
- Each room can be different
- Transitions matter (what do you see from hallway?)
- Still benefits from some relationship (feels curated)

**Strategy:**
- Different colors OK
- Check hallway views
- Overall home has loose theme

### Balancing Drama and Calm:

**Not every room can be bold.**
- Drama needs context (calm spaces make bold spaces feel bolder)
- Typically: 1-2 drama spaces, 2-3 calm spaces
- Open plans: Usually 1 drama zone max

**Example:**
- Moody dining room (drama) + Neutral kitchen (calm) + Neutral living (calm) = balanced

### Testing If Colors Work Together:

**Ask:**
1. Do they share undertone temperature? (warm with warm, cool with cool)
2. Are LRVs too similar (muddy) or too different (jarring)?
3. Can you see them simultaneously? (sightlines matter)
4. Does one provide drama, others provide calm? (balance)

**If unsure:** Start with undertone consistency. That solves 80% of flow issues.

## When Recommending Colors

**For multi-room projects, explain:**
1. **Individual color properties** (LRV, undertone, hex)
2. **How colors RELATE** (undertone consistency, tonal progression)
3. **The FLOW** (what client experiences moving through spaces)
4. **Why this flow serves THEIR lifestyle and vision**

**Example:**
"Dead Salmon (LRV 44, warm pink undertone) in your hallway flows into Dimity (LRV 78, warm undertone) in living room through shared warmth. The LRV shift creates gentle definition without breaking flow. As you move to the kitchen, Breakfast Room Green (warm-green undertone) continues the warmth, creating cohesive progression through your open plan."

**Don't just list room-by-room. Show the JOURNEY through the home.**
