# Material Coordination - Quick Guide

## Core Principle:
**Fixed materials (floors, counters, hardware) drive paint color choices.**  
Match paint undertone to dominant material undertone.

---

## Wood Floors:

**Warm Woods:**
- White oak, red oak, pine (honey, golden tones)
- Need: **Warm** paint undertones
- Avoid: Cool grays, blue-grays (creates harsh clash)

**Cool Woods:**
- Walnut (chocolate with purple undertone)
- Works with: **Cool or neutral** paint undertones

**Check:** Look at wood in natural light. Yellow/orange tones = warm. Purple/gray tones = cool.

---

## Stone & Tile:

**Cool Stone:**
- Carrara marble (blue-gray veining)
- Slate (usually gray-green-blue)
- Need: **Cool or neutral** paint undertones

**Warm Stone:**
- Calacatta marble (gold/brown veining)
- Limestone, travertine (cream, honey)
- Terracotta tile (orange-red)
- Need: **Warm** paint undertones

**Mixed (like slate with rust tones):**
- Use neutral bridge colors that work with both
- OR choose which tone is dominant and match that

---

## Hardware & Metals:

**Warm Metals:**
- Brass, gold, bronze, unlacquered brass
- Need: **Warm** paint undertones

**Cool Metals:**
- Chrome, polished nickel, stainless steel
- Need: **Cool** paint undertones

**Neutral:**
- Matte black, oil-rubbed bronze
- Works with any undertone

---

## When Materials Conflict:

**Example:** Cool marble counters + warm oak floors

**Options:**
1. **Neutral bridge** - Choose paint that doesn't lean strongly either way
2. **Pick dominant** - Match the most prominent/visible material
3. **Discuss tradeoff** - Explain the tension, let client decide

**Don't claim it won't work - explain the tradeoff honestly.**

---

## How to Use:

When recommending colors, **always reference client's actual materials:**

❌ DON'T: "This color works with brass"  
✅ DO: "This warm neutral (LRV 52, warm undertone) harmonizes with YOUR brass hardware and honey oak floors"

**Be specific to THEIR space, not generic advice.**
